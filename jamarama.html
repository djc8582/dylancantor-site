Jamarama

Four phones, one shared clock, one fused track.

RoleAudio architecture and the jam system WithSai Nellutla AwardWinner, Audiotool Challenge — Google DeepMind Magenta Hackathon DateJune 2026

Two to four players each control one dimension of a single track from their phones — drums, harmony, lead, or crowd energy. When one player changes the chord, the whole band re-roots on the next downbeat.

  • Master clock lives in the host browser and fans beats to phones over WebSocket; phones are thin controllers.
  • The lead keyboard fires a local tone in under 20 ms, so playing never waits on a network round trip.
  • I measured the generative model for tempo stability first. It drifts within ~30 seconds — there is no tempo input — so I took it off the clock entirely: a deterministic clock-locked band wrapped in an arhythmic texture layer that floats off the grid, where drift is inaudible.
  • That layer runs Magenta RealTime 2 on-device via MLX at ~2.37× real-time, conditioned on blended player taste, live chord, and crowd energy.

The interesting constraint was never what the model could generate. It was what the model could generate in time — and that answer came from measuring rather than assuming.

Tone.js · WebSocket · Node · Python · MLX
